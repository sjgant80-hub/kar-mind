Kar the controller: own the body, call the cortex

What it is. One rule for every job an AI estate does: send it to the cheapest mind that can do it right. A job an organ already does (run a gate, pack the memory, re-run a census) runs as code, with no model at all. A narrow wording job (summarise, tag, draft a short line) goes to the small model on this laptop. Judgement (propose, decide, review, design) goes to Claude, on the owner's own login. A nightly wall caps what the frontier may spend, checked before every call. When the frontier cannot be reached, the local model does the job and the record says so.

Start here

  1. Route a job. Type any job in plain words, or pick one, and press Route. This is the real controller (controller.mjs, the same file the gate tests), running in your browser.
  2. Hit the wall. Ask for frontier calls against one night's budget and watch the fourth be refused before it is made.
  3. Price your own mix. Further down, put in how many jobs you run a month and how they split; the controller's own cost function prices them at the vendor's published list.

2 · One night's wall. Budget 100,000 tokens; each frontier call reserves its whole ceiling of 30,000 before it is made.

    How it works

    1. The first verb decides. The controller reads the job's first word against three short lists: an organ's verbs (run, check, pack, verify, measure, census…), wording verbs (summarise, tag, draft, rewrite…) and judgement verbs (propose, decide, review, plan, design, diagnose…). A verb it does not know goes to the frontier while the night's budget allows (Claude when unsure), and otherwise to the local model, marked as a fallback.
    2. The wall is checked before the call. A frontier call is made only if its whole ceiling still fits in tonight's budget, and the ceiling is written down as spent before the call goes out, so a night that dies mid-call has still counted it. A call that ever used more than its ceiling is charged the difference afterwards, and once the books are full nothing more goes out until the next night.
    3. The frontier only thinks. The call goes through the vendor's official command-line client, from an empty folder so no project files ride along, with no tools, no servers, nothing saved, and its output capped at 4,000 tokens. It runs on the owner's own login: the controller never holds an API key, and a call that has not answered in 240 seconds is cut.
    4. The fallback says so. If the frontier does not answer, the local model makes the answer and the record names it as a fallback, with the reason. Nothing that did not come from the frontier is ever marked as the frontier's.
    5. Every night, without being asked. Kar's nightly loop on the owner's laptop asks the controller for one build idea from the two things its dream graph found together most often. The idea, its maker, its tokens and its five checks land in the morning report.

    The blueprint

    • controller.mjs, the law: routing, the nightly wallet, the idea's prompt and its five checks, and the cost case. Pure and deterministic; it decides and spends nothing. Witness-gated: every mutant of it is caught by its tests.
    • tools/cortex.mjs, the frontier wire: one call through the official client, from an empty folder, with the environment cleaned of keys and proxies.
    • The local wire picks the biggest local model that fits in free memory, and refuses one that does not, because loaded anyway it pages to disk and crawls.
    • The wallet is the estate's shared foundation (fall-kit/core), vendored verbatim with every file pinned by SHA-256 and checked on every push.
    • tools/controller-measure.mjs, the sealed measurement: --check (the seal is the committed file), --run (only on committed, pushed code), --verify (re-grade every stored reply; CI runs it).
    • Kar's nightly loop, on the owner's laptop, calls the controller once a night and saves the wallet before each call.

    What it does for a business

    Most teams wire AI one way: every job goes to one big rented model, or every job goes to one small one. The first pays frontier prices for work a script or a small model does as well. The second asks a small model for judgement it cannot give, and the sealed measurement above shows how much worse it does. The controller is the switchboard between them, with a ceiling the bill cannot pass.

    Security and data protection

    • What leaves the machine: only the words of a judgement job, sent to the AI vendor the owner already uses, on the owner's own account. Organ jobs and wording jobs stay on the machine.
    • Personal data: the router decides by the verb, not by the content, so a judgement job that contains personal data goes to the frontier. Keep personal data out of judgement jobs, or set the frontier budget to zero and everything stays local (UK GDPR and GDPR: the controller decides what is sent, and the record shows what was).
    • Audit: every nightly answer is appended to a ledger with its maker, its tier, its tokens and the reason for its route. Nothing is rewritten.
    • Keys: none stored by the controller. The client uses the owner's own login, and the environment is cleaned of every key and proxy before each call.

    Roll-out

    1. Week one: run with a frontier budget of zero. Everything runs as code or on the local model, and the record shows where each job would have gone.
    2. Week two: set a nightly budget you are comfortable with. The wall holds it, call by call.
    3. Then: add your organs' verbs to the lists. That is a change to the gated law, so it is tested before it ships.
    4. Every morning: read the report: what was made, by which mind, for how many tokens, and whether it passed its checks.

    Risks, answered straight

    • The router reads the first verb, not the meaning. "check whether the plan is sound" goes to the organ tier, because "check" is an organ's verb. The sealed thirty jobs were written with the lists, so they test that the lists do what they say, not real traffic; every route is in the record, so a misroute is seen and fixed in the lists.
    • The local model is weaker at judgement. That is why judgement goes to the frontier; the measurement above says by how much.
    • The frontier is a rented service. If it is down, or its client changes how it reports, the controller refuses a report it cannot read and the night falls back to the local model, marked.
    • The wall bounds tokens, not quality. The five checks catch an idea that is shapeless, invented, jargon or a repeat; they do not judge whether it is a good idea. The owner does.
    • One client version was measured. The receipt names it; a new version is measured again before its numbers are quoted.

    The case for the board

    AI spend has two failure modes: paying frontier prices for clerical work, and trusting a cheap model with decisions. The controller removes both with a rule anyone can read, a ceiling the bill cannot pass, and a record of which mind made every answer. It was measured against claims written before it existed, the measurement is re-graded on every push, and the code is the owner's to keep.

    The paper: own the body, call the cortex

    An AI estate is mostly body: organs that run, check, pack and count, each one deterministic and gated. Most of what it does needs no model at all, and much of the rest needs only a small one. What it lacks on its own is judgement: choosing what to build next, weighing a design, seeing what two of its parts could become together. The controller keeps the body owned and calls a frontier mind, the cortex, only for that.

    Three rules make it safe to leave running. The route is decided by code, so it can be tested and read. The spend is decided before the call, so a night cannot pass its budget however it ends. And the record never lies about its maker, so an answer the local model made is never mistaken for the frontier's. The measurement was sealed first, so the claims could fail; the ones that did are on this page beside the ones that held.

    The brain is switched on

    Every organ below was built and proven, but they were not joined: a person carried each result from one to the next, so the person was the wiring. Since 2026-10-01 the organs are joined into one loop, and nobody has to relay anything between them:

    1. The journal is Kar's working memory. Every session start reads the memory files, Kar's asks and the build queue into the persistent journal (through the real mind and its sealed, walled event log), and starts the session from the digest it reads back: the latest builds, what changed, the next build, and the decisions only Simon makes. brain.mjs holds that law; brain-cli.mjs is the glue.
    2. The direct line carries it. Every message to Kar's cockpit reads the brain fresh, on every tier, including the local model on this machine.
    3. The soul takes memory in live. A lesson written mid-day is swapped into the running soul at once, chunked as boot chunks it. No restart.
    4. The loop runs from the queue. The next build is the first open item in Simon's approved order whose prerequisites are done. Money, publishing and standing switches are named as his decisions and wait for him; nothing in the brain can act on one.

    The seed's codec against Simon's 15×

    Simon's call: "15x is there". The seed's memory law says compress by prime indices, and its primorial fold codec (a set over prime rings ⇄ one integer, lossless by unique factorisation — the encoding core of Thomas Frumkin's work) is the mechanism. A shared dictionary saves only what the reader already holds: the session start's reader holds the memory index, a running cockpit session holds the digest it was given, claudedidy and Kar both hold the memory index. So every real payload is coded against exactly that, decoded back to prove nothing was lost, and counted in Claude tokens on Simon's login, with his 15× sealed as the bar.

The codec only replaces what the reader already holds. The frontier model still has to read anything new in full — a changed line, a new message, a first turn — and that is most of the workload. The cockpit brain refresh has one real pair today (the watch seal → now); more would come only with time. Prime products (the bloom integer) carry sets between code organs at no Claude cost; a model cannot factor them, so no payload read by a model uses them.

    kar-mind wires small, independently-gated memory organs into one mind. It perceives a fact, cross-checks it against independent sources, consolidates it into meaning, generates new facts and loops them back, and births new domains — through real organs, with every state change read from each organ's own count. Nothing here uses an LLM as a judge; every check is deterministic and re-runnable.

    The organs

    • crystal — exact facts, no drift; a read never blends one source's value into another's.
    • octa — verify: cross-checks 3+ independent sources per field, refuses a panel of duplicates.
    • strand — the movement law: recall score, then promote / hold / decay / expire, then the shelf transition.
    • dodeca — meaning: cosine-embedding retrieval.
    • icosa — generate: a dream cycle infers new facts from repeated patterns and loops the best back.
    • tetra — birth: bounded new-domain detection (novelty threshold + hard capacity cap).

    It survives a restart

    Cognition is event-sourced: every state-changing verb records its cause. The log is sealed to disk with real gzip and a receipt hash over every field (tamper-refused on load), and on boot the organs replay it and reach identical counts — a write-ahead-log persistence architecture. A brand-new process boots from the durable file alone and comes back whole.

    It runs its own loop

    From one call, the run-loop pulls its own stimuli and drives the full cascade over and over, moving real organ state with no caller per step — governed by a safety cap, stopping on dead air rather than spinning, and persisting itself each beat.

    The safety wall. The autonomous loop can perceive, verify, consolidate, dream, and route — all cognition, all reversible, all inside the mind's own walled memory. It cannot publish or spend: the loop has no path to any irreversible outside-world action, by construction. The export door stays doubly gated by a consent id and a path wall, and a test proves a full autonomous run writes no file.
